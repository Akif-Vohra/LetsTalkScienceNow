Ramgarh is India's **third confirmed meteorite impact structure**, alongside [Lonar](/stories/lonar-crater/) and the far older [Dhala](/stories/dhala-impact-structure/). It sits in the Vindhyan hills of south-east Rajasthan as a striking **near-circular ring of raised sandstone**, roughly 3–4 km across — which for decades was read as just an unusual set of hills. The village and a hilltop temple sit inside the bowl.

The impact reading was clinched by the tell-tale signatures a passing asteroid leaves in rock: **shatter cones**, **planar deformation features in quartz**, and iron-rich spherules enriched in nickel and cobalt. A 2020 study argued the visible ring is the eroded **central uplift of a larger, ~10 km complex crater**, and Ramgarh was recognised that year as India's third — and Rajasthan's only — confirmed impact site.

Its **age is genuinely unsettled**. The target is Neoproterozoic Vindhyan (Bhander Group) sandstone, so the impact must be younger than that; recent U–Pb work on shocked zircons points to roughly **530–395 million years ago**, while other studies have argued for a much younger (~165 Ma) or older age. The site is now being developed as a **geo-heritage destination**.
