The Indus rises near Mount Kailash on the Tibetan plateau and flows northwest through the high, arid valleys of **Ladakh** — closely shadowing the **Indus–Tsangpo suture**, the tectonic weld where India collided with Asia — before bending south toward the Arabian Sea.

It is one of the great rivers of the subcontinent and gives **India its name**; along its lower plains grew the Bronze-Age cities of the **Indus Valley (Harappan) civilisation**, among the earliest urban cultures on Earth.
