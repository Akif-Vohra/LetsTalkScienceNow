Angadipuram is the **type locality of laterite** — the iron- and aluminium-rich, brick-red rock that hardens once it is dug out and exposed to air. The Scottish physician **Francis Buchanan-Hamilton named it here in 1807**, from the Latin *later*, "brick"; the term is now used the world over. The Geological Survey of India has declared the site a **National Geological Monument**.

Laterite forms by **intense chemical weathering** in hot, wet climates: rain strips silica and soluble bases out of the underlying rock and leaves a residue of iron and aluminium oxides behind. Across Kerala and the Western Ghats it is still cut in neat blocks straight from the ground as a traditional building stone.
