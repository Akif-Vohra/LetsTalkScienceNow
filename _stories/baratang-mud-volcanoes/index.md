Baratang has **India's only mud volcanoes** — low cones and pools of cool grey mud that bubble, gurgle and now and then erupt, known locally as *jalki*. Despite the name they are **not igneous**: gas- and water-charged mud is squeezed upward along faults from deep, over-pressured sediments, with no molten rock involved.

That plumbing is a by-product of the **Andaman subduction zone**, where the Indian Plate dives beneath the Burma Plate and scrapes off a thick, water-logged **accretionary wedge** of sediment. The same tectonic setting raised the islands and fuels the molten volcanism of nearby [Barren Island](/stories/barren-island/) — mud here, magma there.
