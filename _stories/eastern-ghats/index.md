The Eastern Ghats are a broken, discontinuous range of ancient **high-grade metamorphic rock** along India's east coast. Because most of the original mountain range has long since eroded away, the rocks now exposed once lay **deep beneath it** — subjected to immense heat and pressure that gave them their striking textures, mineral assemblages, and appearance.

Their signature rock is **khondalite** — named after the **Khond** people (found across Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Chhattisgarh) — a rare high-grade metamorphic rock also found in the **Rayner Complex of East Antarctica**. The two share rock ages, mineral and geochemical signatures, isotopic signatures and metamorphic histories: strong evidence they were once part of a single ancient mountain belt, the **Rayner–Eastern Ghats Orogen**, when India and East Antarctica were fused together in the supercontinents **Rodinia** and, later, **Gondwana**. Rocks now separated by more than 8,000 km were once the same landscape, nearly a billion years ago.

Khondalite's true aesthetic shows in landmark architecture — the **Jagannath Temple** in Puri and the UNESCO-listed **Konark Sun Temple**. And the Ghats still shape their region today: recharging groundwater, steering rainfall through orographic effects, and sheltering diverse tropical and subtropical forests.
