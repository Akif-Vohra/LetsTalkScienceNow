Scattered across the desert near Jaisalmer lie the **petrified trunks of a Jurassic forest** — trees that grew about **180 million years ago**, when this corner of the Thar was a warm, well-watered coast on the edge of Gondwana. Buried in sediment and slowly mineralised, their wood was replaced grain-for-grain by silica, freezing the growth rings in stone. The site is a **National Geological Monument**.

The logs sit within the **Jurassic rocks of the Jaisalmer Basin**, which also preserve marine limestones and shelly fossils — a record that the sea flooded and withdrew from this now-arid land more than once.
