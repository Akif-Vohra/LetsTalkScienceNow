Akal Wood Fossil Park sits in the desert about 18 kilometres southeast of Jaisalmer, and it is exactly what the name says: a scatter of tree trunks turned to stone. Around a dozen fossil logs lie where they came to rest on the sandy ground, the largest more than 13 metres long. The trees grew in the Early Jurassic, roughly 180 million years ago, and the site is protected as a National Geological Monument.

The logs are not wood any more; they are rock in the shape of wood. The process is called petrification. When a fallen tree is buried quickly in sediment, it is sealed away from the air and the microbes that would normally rot it. Mineral-rich groundwater, carrying dissolved silica (the mineral of quartz and sand), then seeps through the buried trunk, filling its pore spaces and replacing much of the original material while preserving the structure. The shape is copied as it goes, down to the growth rings, so what remains is stone that still holds the form of the tree. It is the same slow change that preserved the [Sathanur log](/stories/sathanur-fossil-wood/) far to the south.

The trees themselves were gymnosperms, the conifer-like plants that ruled the land long before flowers existed. They grew in a warm, humid lowland bordering the sea, on the edge of Gondwana, the great southern supercontinent that India still belonged to at the time.

The logs also carry a story about the sea. They lie in sediments laid down by rivers and on floodplains, in a landscape that bordered the sea. The overlying rocks include marine limestones rich in fossils, so the stack, continental sediments followed by marine sediments, is the signature of a coast being drowned: through the Jurassic the sea advanced across this part of Rajasthan, and over time the shoreline swung back and forth. The petrified logs and the shelly limestone together mark where land met sea.

Today the fossil park is fenced and protected. Its stone logs are a direct window onto a Jurassic coast, the remains of gymnosperm trees that grew, fell, and turned to rock while the dinosaurs were still early in their long reign.
