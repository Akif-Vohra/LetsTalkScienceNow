Meghalaya holds **India's longest cave systems** — among them **Krem Liat Prah** in the Jaintia Hills, surveyed to more than 30 km of passages. These are **karst caves**: tunnels dissolved out of thick beds of **Cretaceous–Eocene limestone** by faintly acidic rainwater working along cracks and bedding planes over hundreds of thousands of years.

What makes this corner of India a caving superpower is simply **rain**. The Khasi and Jaintia Hills take the monsoon head-on — Mawsynram and Cherrapunji are among the wettest inhabited places on Earth — and all that water pouring through soluble rock keeps carving an underground world that cavers are still mapping today.
