The Indus–Tsangpo Suture Zone marks the **tectonic seam** where the Indian plate collided with Eurasia and stuck fast, beginning around 50 million years ago. It is, quite literally, the line along which two continents were joined.

Trapped along this suture are **ophiolites** — slabs of oceanic crust and mantle — together with marine sediments. These are the remains of the **Tethys Ocean** that once lay between India and Asia and was consumed as the two landmasses closed together. Finding the floor of a former ocean stranded in the high, dry mountains of Ladakh is one of the clearest signatures of the collision that raised the Himalaya.
