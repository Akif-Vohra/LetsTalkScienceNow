Lonar is a **meteorite impact crater**, blasted out when a space rock slammed into the thick basalt lava of the Deccan Traps during the Pleistocene — on the order of tens of thousands of years ago, though its exact age is still debated. It is scientifically prized because it is **one of the few well-preserved impact craters formed in basaltic rock**, which makes it a useful analogue for studying craters on other rocky, basalt-covered worlds.

Today the crater cradles a **saline, alkaline lake**, and the wetland is recognised internationally as a Ramsar site.
