For about a century, Assam was India's leading oil-producing state — and for the first five decades after the first commercially successful well was drilled in **1890**, it was the *only* state producing oil in India. Digboi's refinery, running since 1901, is the **oldest continuously operating refinery in the world**.

The easy accessibility of that oil is thanks to Assam's unique geology, sandwiched between two major tectonic boundaries: the **Indian–Eurasian** plate boundary to the north and the **Indian–Burmese** boundary to the east. The **Assam–Arakan Basin** was ocean floor for much of the **Eocene and Oligocene**, when large amounts of organic matter accumulated on the seafloor; massive erosion of the rising Himalaya then buried it beneath thick layers of sediment, and deep underground, rising heat and pressure matured it into petroleum.

The oil became far more *accessible* because the continued movement of the Indian Plate beneath the Burmese Plate folded the rock layers into **anticlines** (upward folds), where oil naturally accumulates. Near the **Naga Thrust**, these rocks were also broken and stacked on top of one another, forming the **Naga Schuppen Belt**.

*Simplified for clarity: petroleum actually forms in **source** rocks and migrates into **reservoir** rocks where it accumulates — it doesn't sit in fixed layers.*
